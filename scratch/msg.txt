analyse: the win-probability strip, every seat at every step

The analyser answers "was THIS move a mistake" and carries a running win
probability as a by-product of the analysed seat's own column. The other
question a finished game invites is "who was winning, and when", for every
seat at once, and that is a different shape of work: one board per STEP
rather than one per decision, no candidates, and every seat scored from the
same playout.

cnitro_winprob (make -C c winprob) is that pass, over the analyser's own
pieces rather than a second copy of them - the real rebuild, analyse_belief,
its world sampler and installer, and the playout. It measures two things,
because "chance of winning" is two questions: the position as it really was
(the rebuilt board holds every hidden hand and the real stock, one playout
scores every seat, and the seats' fool probabilities sum to one), and the
same number inside each seat's own information set (what it could honestly
know, which sums to nothing and is meant to).

an_playout is now two calls: analyse_playout_board, the one "play this board
out with real bots" there is, and the finish-position read beside it. Both
are exported, so the strip does not own a second playout loop that could
drift from the analyser's.

The tool checks its own fold: one playout has exactly one fool, so a
position's truth probabilities sum to one up to each seat's rounding, and it
says so on every run.

c/tools/winprob/plot_winprob.py draws the two panels from the TSV.
docs/POST_GAME_ANALYSER.md carries the section, including the measured cost -
the bill is front-loaded, because a playout from the opening plays 145
decisions and one from the last bout plays two.
