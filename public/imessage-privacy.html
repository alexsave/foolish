Privacy Policy

Foolish Cards for iMessage (shown as “Foolish” in Messages)

Effective date: August 13, 2026

The short version: this app collects no data. It has no servers, no accounts, no analytics, no ads, and no third-party code. Your moves travel as iMessages, the same way your texts do, and we never see them.

What this app is

Foolish Cards for iMessage is a two-player Durak card game played entirely inside Apple’s Messages app. It is a standalone iMessage app — there is no separate Home Screen app behind it.

Data we collect

None. Specifically, the app has:

  • no accounts and no sign-in,
  • no analytics, usage tracking, or crash reporting,
  • no ads and no third-party SDKs of any kind,
  • no access to your contacts, location, photos, or anything else on your device beyond its own game data,
  • no in-app purchases.

The app makes no network connections of its own, and we do not operate any server for it. There is nothing on our end that could receive your data, so none of it is collected, stored, shared, or sold — by us or by anyone on our behalf.

How your game moves travel

Each move you make is encoded directly into the iMessage bubble the app sends. That message — the cards played and the nickname you choose to type into the game, if any — is delivered by Apple’s iMessage service exactly like an ordinary text and is visible only to the people in your conversation. Apple’s handling of iMessage is described in Apple’s privacy policy.

The app never sees who your contacts are. Messages identifies conversation participants to the app only by anonymous identifiers; the names shown in the game are the nicknames players type in themselves.

What stays on your device

The app keeps a small local record of your games — which games you are in, which seat is yours, and the latest game state — so it can show your list of games in the Messages drawer. This lives only on your device and is removed when you delete the app.

Children

The app collects no data from anyone, children included.

Changes to this policy

If the app’s behavior ever changes, we will update this page and its effective date. Since the app collects nothing, there is nothing to opt out of today.
