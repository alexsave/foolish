The name is a placeholder and the reason matters. UNO is a Mattel trademark, filed 1974 and enforced recently enough that a clone called UYES was pulled from Google Play by a DMCA takedown. What is not protectable is the game: this is the Crazy Eights family, which is public domain. What is protected is the name, the card art, the four-colour-only suits and the call-out word - so none of those are here. Suits are shape AND colour, which keeps us clear of the trade dress and is also the only version a colourblind player can read.

How it moves, before how it looks

Filled in first. foolish's animation catalogue was written at build 24, after every shape in it had been found by shipping something that felt wrong - and this game's settlement half is far bigger than foolish's, because one card can skip a player, turn the table around and hand somebody two cards.

One fork worth settling now

Does the app announce one card left, or does somebody have to catch it? The call-out is the best moment in the physical game and it exists because a human can forget. An app cannot forget, so announcing it automatically deletes the mechanic and leaves a notification.

The version that survives asynchrony: the app says nothing, and any other player may tap Caught you until the next bubble seals. One bubble is the window. It keeps the tension, it needs no clock, it cannot be lost to a slow phone - and it is the only simultaneous-action mechanic in the whole design, which is exactly why the conflict row above is not empty.
