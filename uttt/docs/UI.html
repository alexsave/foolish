Ultimate Tic-Tac-Toe · surface study · 20 September 2026

Your move picks their board

No kernel yet and no Swift - this is the drawing you argue with first. Every screen is at the size Messages actually gives: 390pt expanded, 340pt collapsed, 274pt in the transcript. The marks are drawn strokes rather than glyphs, so the motion on this page is the real motion.

Why it fits, in bytes

81 cells at 2 bits is 162 bits. Add the forced board (4), the side to move (1), and nine board verdicts (18) and the whole game is about 25 bytes - against a body budget of roughly 625. That is four per cent, with no compression and nothing dropped.

Werewolf needed 1,323 base32 characters for a 32-player night and had to throw away old transcripts to fit. This fits thirty times over. A game of 40 to 60 moves, and every bubble carries the entire game - so there is no chain to walk and no state a late tap cannot rebuild.

How it moves, before how it looks

Filled in first, on purpose. The animation catalogue for foolish was written at build 24, after every shape in it had been found by shipping something that felt wrong. One move has seven channels; a cell that says nothing moves is a decision, an empty cell is not.

ChannelWhen What moves
A stagedI tap a square, before Send The mark draws (260/340ms). Then the destination board pulses, at +300ms. Drawer auto-collapses once the ink lands, never during.
B at SendI tap the native arrow Usually nothing - A already drew it. If the move took a small board, the strike and the fill play here: that is the settlement half.
C my replayI reopen my own bubble Both halves, from the position before my move. Should not surprise me, so it runs once and does not pulse.
D their bubbleI open a bubble of theirs Both halves plus the pulse. This is the common case - most bubbles you open are somebody else's, and the pulse is the only thing that tells you where you have been sent.
E arrivalTheir move lands while I am looking Mark draws in, boards re-dim, destination pulses. The only channel that interrupts something you were already reading.
Undox on the staged bubble, or the pill The stroke un-inks in reverse order, 200ms, and the highlight returns to the board it was on. No auto-collapse - an undo must not move the drawer.
Conflict— Cannot happen, and this is a decision rather than an omission. Strict alternation means there is exactly one player to move, so two moves can never race. A duplicate or an older bubble is Rule P's problem, and Rule P animates nothing.
