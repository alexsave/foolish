Bone dice on a drowned table

Every piece the game draws, one at a time, at real size, in the register of the five frames the owner sent: a hold with no daylight, one cold green light from above, cups gone to verdigris, dice cut from bone and yellowed in brine, and the only warmth on the table a dull blood red where a 1 sits. One tab at a time. The die and the cup come first, each with a bank of materials, silhouettes and details and a mixer to combine them; the rest of the pieces wait under their tabs until those two are settled.

The frames, read for colour

Five stills, quantised to their seven heaviest colours each. The surprise is how little of the frame is anything but black: in four of the five, 84 to 96 percent of the picture is under #0f1a18. The look is not teal; it is black with teal in it.

Why only one bright colour

The old table had brass for the turn, amber for the catch ring, red for Caught, gold for selection. In the dark all four fight. Here glow is the one bright thing and it means exactly "this is the live one": the seat whose turn it is, the dice that count at a reveal, the face chip you chose, Raise when it is legal. It is the light of something alive down there. Blood is the only other saturated colour and it means loss: the wild pip (a 1 counts against you as much as for you), Liar, and the stamp. Everything else is bone, bronze and water.

Materials

The five base textures, all baked by the kernel at the foot of this page (the die and cup tabs add their own palettes to the same generator): a seeded integer hash, value noise on a lattice that wraps so the tile stitches, and fbm over it. Each one is a loop per texel with a handful of named amplitudes, which is exactly the shape of FeltTexture.Palette, so the port is a transliteration and not a redesign. The bake times are measured live, below each tile.

Why these are procedural and not photographs

Same reason the felt and the wool are: a tile that is written as numbers has no licence, no author, no resolution, and no "where did this come from" in a store review. Every one of these has three to five amplitudes and one seed. The deck is the felt generator with one more pass (the thresholded flecks); the bone is the felt generator with a ridge pass for the veins; the verdigris is a bronze cloud with two smoothstepped masks over it; the sea is one ridged fbm raised to a power; the crust is not noise at all but a seeded scatter of discs, each a radial gradient with a hole, which is a for loop over CGContext.drawRadialGradient.

The die

A die is three decisions: what it is cut from, what silhouette the cutter left, and what the pips are. Round two settled most of them. Locked: the machined silhouette (a rounded square, radius 18%), the bevel, the drilled pip, medium at 21% of the side, placed a little off exact (1.2% of the side, between exact and hand-drilled), and the wild 1 in blood. Open: the material, six bakes, with the yellowed and the crazed bone combined into one as asked (and the crazing lighter, thinner and sparser); and the strike wear. Dropped: chips. In their place, the owner’s idea: a die hits the table on its corners and edges, so that is where it discolours.

What to look for at 22, not at 44

The 44 row is the one you will stare at; the 22 row is the one the game shows most, under another seat's name on a short board. A material that reads at 44 and turns to mud at 22 is out. A pip style whose hole still reads at 22 is in.

Strike wear: a warm grime feathered in from each corner and along each edge, the corners deepest because they hit first. Every corner has its own size and depth and every edge its own depth, by seed, so no two dice are worn alike, and the same die is worn the same way for a whole game. The isometric row is where it shows: the wear of three faces meets along the cube’s edges and gathers at its corners. "Marked" is the same at 1.7 times the depth, for comparison.

The wild 1 is the only pip with a colour, and it is blood.

The roll

As it goes in life. My cup is held up, mouth to the ceiling, and I look down into it at my five dice. Held leaning toward my face, as a player holds it to look in, for two seconds it shakes hard, sideways, up and down and tilting, and the dice knock about inside. Then, still shaking, it turns over in four tenths of a second and slams down on the planks, and the dice are under it where they fell. Nothing is revealed, so nothing has to be hidden: the faces never change, because the throw is the roll, and what came up is read off the up faces under the cup. Send sends that hand; Roll again throws again; a tap on the landed cup peeks. And every seat throws (round twenty): each far cup is baked by the same kernel with its own geometry, its own seed, its own shake length and its own start, a tenth to six tenths of a second after mine, so no two shake alike or land together; a small cup’s throw is mine in miniature (the reach and gravity scale with it, the clock does not), and the kernel starts every turn on the bob’s beat, because that is what keeps the dice in at any shake length. It plays as the tab opens, at real size, on the layout tab’s seats.

And it is the kernel’s. The throw is C now: chuiniu/c/src/cn_roll.c runs the whole simulation once, in advance, and hands back every frame of it, a position and a quaternion for the cup and each die; the host plays the frames and integrates nothing. What plays below is that C compiled to WebAssembly and embedded in this file; the line under each screen is the kernel reporting what the bake cost.

The physics a cup that moves, five cubes that answer

The cup is kinematic: its pose is a function of the clock, and its speed and spin are read from the pose a step earlier. The dice are rigid cubes (position, velocity, a rotation matrix whose columns are the cube’s axes, an angular velocity; a cube’s inertia is the same about every axis, m d² / 6). Each step of a 240th of a second, every corner is tested against three surfaces: the table, the cup’s floor (a shallow dome, as a cup’s bottom is) and the cup’s wall (a cone, tested in the cup’s own frame); a contact impulse with restitution and Coulomb friction is taken against the surface’s own velocity, so a wall coming at a die hits it and a floor pulling away lets it fall; the dice push each other apart by bounding spheres. The cup has a real inside: the wall’s thickness at the rim, the inner wall darker the deeper it goes, the domed floor; the peek shows it too.

Keeping them in is the throw, not a rule. The flip turns about a point a sixth of the way in from the mouth, where a hand grips a cup, so the floor swings on a long arm and the dice are pressed to it most of the way over; the turn gathers speed (as the square of its progress, a wrist and not a whip) and stops dead, and the cup is driven down at 1.8 g, timed so the mouth meets the planks the moment it faces them, faster than the dice can fall. The shake carries on through the turn and fades only over the drop, so the cup lands flat. Every number was searched: a half-second turn is the slowest that keeps the dice in (0.55 s leaves a corner 3 points from the mouth; 0.6 s spills) and four tenths is the one used; the shake, 21 points sideways and 21 up and down at four beats a second with a tilt of 17° either way, is the hardest the cup holds; and the cup grew from 1.8 to 2.1 of its radius tall to hold it. At the slam the cup stops and the dice do not, and they bounce about under it until they settle. A die that lies on another is shivered loose after a second, as a knuckle on the cup would. Round twenty-two: gravity is always on: the dice sit settled on the cup’s floor from the first frame (a half second of unseen settling before it), and each cup rises from low over the table to the held height over the first .35 s of its hold, eased so nothing jolts the dice, so a seat whose throw waits its turn shows a cup low and dice at rest, never dice in the air. The corners’ order in the contacts is flipped with the step and the die: taken in one fixed order, the first corner took the most of every landing and the +x and +y faces came up a seventh too often over 1200 seeds; now the faces are even.

Measured by cn_roll_test (make -C chuiniu/c run), over 300 seeds: every throw completes; no die corner comes within 12 points of the mouth before the slam; every die ends flat on the table under the cup, none on another, none placed by the last resort; the slowest settle is 2 s after the slam. The face up before the turn comes up again 17% of the time and its opposite 19% (a fair 17% each), and every face comes up as often: χ² 8 over five degrees for 1,500 dice, and 10 for 4,500. That last number found a bug: the integrator’s orthonormalisation handed each step’s error to one axis of the die, and over a throw that favoured the other two faces by a sixth; it is symmetric now. A bake is about 2 ms natively and a few milliseconds here, for 200 to 320 frames: 55 to 90 KB of poses that the host could keep or throw away. The same seed bakes the same bytes on every machine (the trig is the kernel’s own series, the build is -ffp-contract=off, one random draw a statement), checked bit for bit between gcc, clang and this module over 60 seeds, and a pinned golden holds the recipe.

What it decides. The bake says which face of each die is up; it knows no pip counts. So the hand can be the physics’ own (what this page shows: the value already on the up face), or the kernel’s K2 dice painted onto the up faces, which keeps every rule about derivation as it is. Either is one line; the owner’s call. The bubble carries the hand, not the throw: nobody replays it.

Why this face decision

The frames are a thing that has been under water for a very long time; a clean modern sans on them looks like a HUD. A Fell type has the right wear built into the glyphs themselves, so no distressing filter is needed (and none would port). The small caps carry names and headers so the roman is kept for the words that matter: the bid and the ask.

Numerals: the Fell roman has old-style figures that sit below the line, which is right for "three 3s" in running text and wrong for a stepper. The quantity numeral is set a size up so its old-style 3 has room, and tabular-nums so 9 to 10 does not jump.

The Chinese is Noto Serif TC, also OFL. On iOS the system's Songti is the fallback and is close enough for a two-character lockup.

Minimum sizes. Fell's x-height is small. Nothing below 14 in small caps and 15.5 in roman; the 11pt headers survive only because they are tracked at .34em and used for five-word labels.

Open, and not guessed at

What this borrows, and what it must not

The owner’s call, second round: no pirate theming by name (no sea-dog captions, no product’s cup or dice art, and LEGAL.md’s list of names stands), but the visual design may go where the frames go. So the materials are the materials of a drowned thing: brine, barnacles, verdigris, bone, tar, iron, horn and one cold light. Nothing is traced, and the words on the table stay the kernel’s own. LEGAL.md’s theming paragraph still says "no pirate theming" without that distinction; it needs the one sentence that makes it.

Everything is drawn so it can be ported: the textures are baked by a seeded per-texel loop (the shape of FeltTexture.render), the shapes are paths and gradients, and every glow is a stroke plus a shadow. The port map tab has the table.

For the owner

Light mode. There is none here. The frames have no daylight, and a drowned table in light mode is a different design, not a palette swap. The proposal is one appearance, dark, as the werewolf night is.

The old-style figures. Fell's 3 and 5 descend, its 5 at small sizes looks like a long s (see the count stamped on a 32pt cup), and its 1 is a small capital I: "12" in the quantity above reads as "I2" until you know. If that reads wrong at a glance on the device, the fallback is lining figures from the system serif for the quantity and the stamped count only; the words ("three 3s") keep Fell's.

Reduce motion. The caustic drift is the only motion in these elements. It should honour the system setting and stand still.

Not here. The layout (where the seats go, the plate, the drawer's three heights), the motion grid, and the bubble's picture are the next document, built from these pieces.
