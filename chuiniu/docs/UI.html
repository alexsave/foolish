Bone dice on a drowned table

Every piece the game draws, one at a time, at real size, in the register of the five frames the owner sent: a hold with no daylight, one cold green light from above, cups gone to verdigris, dice cut from bone and yellowed in brine, and the only warmth on the table a dull blood red where a 1 sits. Layout is a later document; this one settles what the pieces are made of, so the layout can only arrange them.

What this borrows, and what it must not

LEGAL.md rules out pirate theming by name: no ships, skulls, parrots, cutlasses, treasure, no sea-dog captions, and no published product's cup or dice art. Everything here stays on the generic side of that line: brine, barnacles, verdigris, bone, deep water and one cold light are the materials of any drowned thing, not of one film. There is no hat, no tentacle, no hull and no skull anywhere in it, and nothing is traced. The dice are a plain cube with drilled pips; the cup is a plain tapered cup. If the owner takes this direction, LEGAL.md's theming paragraph needs one sentence saying so, because "dark aquatic" is a step closer to the film than "casino green" was.

Everything is drawn so it can be ported: the four textures are baked by a seeded per-texel loop (the shape of FeltTexture.render), the shapes are paths and gradients (SwiftUI Path, LinearGradient, RadialGradient), and every glow is a stroke plus a shadow. Section 12 is the map.

The frames, read for colour

Five stills, quantised to their seven heaviest colours each. The surprise is how little of the frame is anything but black: in four of the five, 84 to 96 percent of the picture is under #0f1a18. The look is not teal; it is black with teal in it.

Two faces. 91% near-black; drowned skin #5b684e, kelp #535132, water #2d4636.

Three things to carry: the bone is cold (its shadows go green, never brown); the pips are the darkest thing in any frame; and the water reads as a light source behind things, not as a colour painted on them.

Why only one bright colour

The old table had brass for the turn, amber for the catch ring, red for Caught, gold for selection. In the dark all four fight. Here glow is the one bright thing and it means exactly "this is the live one": the seat whose turn it is, the dice that count at a reveal, the face chip you chose, Raise when it is legal. It is the light of something alive down there. Blood is the only other saturated colour and it means loss: the wild pip (a 1 counts against you as much as for you), Call, and the stamp. Everything else is bone, bronze and water.

Materials

Five textures, all baked by the kernel at the foot of this page: a seeded integer hash, value noise on a lattice that wraps so the tile stitches, and fbm over it. Each one is a loop per texel with a handful of named amplitudes, which is exactly the shape of FeltTexture.Palette, so the port is a transliteration and not a redesign. The bake times are measured live, below each tile.

Why these are procedural and not photographs

Same reason the felt and the wool are: a tile that is written as numbers has no licence, no author, no resolution, and no "where did this come from" in a store review. Every one of these has three to five amplitudes and one seed. The deck is the felt generator with one more pass (the thresholded flecks); the bone is the felt generator with a ridge pass for the veins; the verdigris is a bronze cloud with two smoothstepped masks over it; the sea is one ridged fbm raised to a power; the crust is not noise at all but a seeded scatter of discs, each a radial gradient with a hole, which is a for loop over CGContext.drawRadialGradient.

The die

A cube of bone, slightly yellowed, with pips that are holes and not dots. The one rule it knows is the game's: a 1 is wild, and its pip is blood. The pip table is Die.pips unchanged; what changes is the material, the hole, and the two states a reveal puts a die into.

The six faces at 44 die

Pips are 21% of the side for 2 to 6 and 26% for the lone 1, a shade bigger than the shipped 20/24 because a hole needs its rim. Each pip's gradient is offset toward the bottom right: the far wall of the hole catches the light.

At 32 and 22 sizes

32 is the reveal row and the face chip; 22 is a seat's own dice on a short board. The hole still reads at 22 because the rim gradient is a quarter of the pip, not a hairline.

Counting and drowned reveal

At a reveal the kernel flags which dice count (Reveal.counts). A counting die wears the glow as a ring with a blur under it (stroke plus .shadow); a die that does not count is drowned: a sea-coloured wash at 55% and the whole die at 70%, so it sinks instead of fading.

Worn, blank, and mid-tumble character

A chip is a pale wedge at one corner (fresh bone under the yellow skin) with a dark hairline, chosen per die by a seed so the same die is always the same die. A blank face is a die not yet known. The tumble is three faces of one cube in isometric shade, the frame the roll passes through.

The cup

The same tapered silhouette as CupShape, in bronze gone to verdigris, with barnacles grown up from the mouth. The count badge is a disc of bone hung on the lip, because the number of dice under a cup is the one thing about another seat you are allowed to know.

Mouth down, at 44 and 64 cup

The verdigris bake under a horizontal light (lit left flank, deep shadow right) and a vertical one (the crown catches the lamp). The crust is the barnacle tile clipped to the cup and masked to its lower half, so the growth is heaviest where the cup met the deck.

Why this face decision

The frames are a thing that has been under water for a very long time; a clean modern sans on them looks like a HUD. A Fell type has the right wear built into the glyphs themselves, so no distressing filter is needed (and none would port). The small caps carry names and headers so the roman is kept for the words that matter: the bid and the ask.

Numerals: the Fell roman has old-style figures that sit below the line, which is right for "three 3s" in running text and wrong for a stepper. The quantity numeral is set a size up so its old-style 3 has room, and tabular-nums so 9 to 10 does not jump.

The Chinese is Noto Serif TC, also OFL. On iOS the system's Songti is the fallback and is close enough for a two-character lockup.

Minimum sizes. Fell's x-height is small. Nothing below 14 in small caps and 15.5 in roman; the 11pt headers survive only because they are tracked at .34em and used for five-word labels.

Open, and not guessed at

For the owner

The legal line. Section 0 says why this stays generic. The owner decides whether "drowned" is far enough from the film, and if so amends LEGAL.md's theming paragraph; if not, the materials still work on a plain dark deck with the crust removed.

Light mode. There is none here. The frames have no daylight, and a drowned table in light mode is a different design, not a palette swap. The proposal is one appearance, dark, as the werewolf night is.

The old-style figures. Fell's 3 and 5 descend, and its 1 is a small capital I: "12" in the quantity above reads as "I2" until you know. If that reads wrong at a glance on the device, the fallback is lining figures from the system serif for the quantity and the badge only; the words ("three 3s") keep Fell's.

Reduce motion. The caustic drift is the only motion in these elements. It should honour the system setting and stand still.

Not here. The layout (where the seats go, the plate, the drawer's three heights), the motion grid, and the bubble's picture are the next document, built from these pieces.
