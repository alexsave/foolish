# Game Boy Durak: sound design

Alex's brief: "take what the real foolish website does, and convert it to 8 bit audio or whatever the GB can handle."
This file is the design for that: what the DMG can do, how sound shares the machine with the bots, which website cue becomes what Game Boy voice, the two ways to make the card sounds (synthesis and PCM) compared with numbers, the driver, and how the build phase will test it.

Written 2026-11-21, before any ROM build: everything here was measured offline, on a model of the APU (`host/apu.c`) running the real driver (`snd.c`) and tables (`snd_tab.c`).
Built since: `README.md` "Sound" in `gameboy/` has the build, its measurements, and where it departs from this (a landing held on the frame clock, not struck its pre-roll early; the state as arrays; the tick with interrupts on; INTO_HAND at priority 2; two races fixed).
The final judge is Alex by ear; an mGBA render of the real ROM comes in the build phase, and it overrides any number here that disagrees with it.

## Hypotheses (written before measuring) and how they came out

H1. The website's slides, scrapes and grabs (DEAL, LIFT, TURN, PASS, the strokes, INTO_HAND) have at least 30% of their energy above 4 kHz, so 4-bit PCM at 8192 Hz (Nyquist 4096 Hz) loses most of what makes them; the landings (PLACE, COVER, DEAL_LAND, FLIP, PILE) have under 15% there.
Result: mostly held.
DEAL 55%, TURN 64%, INTO_HAND 62%, the strokes 48%, PASS 39% are above 30%; LIFT (21%), PICKUP (19%) and SWEEP (27%) are not.
Every landing is under 11% (PLACE 0.5%, FLIP 2.8%, DEAL_LAND 6.9%, PILE 7.4%, COVER 10.5%).

H2. Fitted noise synthesis driven at 241 Hz matches the takes' 5 ms envelopes within 3 dB on average; driven at 60 Hz it is at least 2 dB worse on the landings.
Result: falsified twice.
At 241 Hz the mean envelope error is 4.1 dB on the landings and 5.4 dB on the rest; at 60 Hz it is 4.5 and 5.8, only 0.4 dB worse, because the length counter cuts a landing inside its tick.
So the driver runs at 60 Hz, on the timer interrupt the game already has (see "60 Hz or 241 Hz").

H3. 4-bit PCM at 8192 Hz matches the landings' envelopes within 2 dB and their spectra within 3 dB below 4 kHz, and misses the slides' spectra by more than 6 dB above 4 kHz.
Result: half held.
Landings: envelope 3.5 dB (not within 2), spectrum below 4 kHz 2.2 dB (within 3).
The rest above 4 kHz: 6.3 dB on average, 6.5 to 7.4 dB for PASS, SWEEP and INTO_HAND.

H4. ROM: the synthesis tables, three takes a card cue, under 6 KB; PCM of the same takes about 4 KB a second and 25 to 40 KB in all.
Result: the tables are 4,962 bytes (held); the PCM is 45,660 bytes (over by 14%: the windows are longer than I guessed).

H5. RAM: the driver's state under 64 bytes, and overlaid on the search bots' scratch it costs no work RAM.
Result: held.
SDCC puts it at 58 bytes (`_DATA 0x3A` for `snd.c`): 53 of state that can be overlaid and 5 that cannot (below).

H6. CPU: under 150 M-cycles a tick while sounding, nothing during a think; CH3 PCM 8% to 15% of the CPU while it plays.
Result: the first part falsified, by estimate.
SDCC compiles `snd_tick` to 281 instructions and `write_row` to 293; the path of a tick with one row change is about 430 instructions, about 1,000 M-cycles (5.7% of a frame) while a cue sounds.
The idle path (nothing sounding, or a bot thinking) is 6 instructions, about 27 M-cycles with the call.
The PCM estimate (9.8% through GBDK's dispatcher, 3.7% with an assembly handler of its own) held.
The build phase measures both on the scanline clock (see "Tests").

## The DMG's sound hardware

Sources: Pan Docs, https://gbdev.io/pandocs/Audio.html, https://gbdev.io/pandocs/Audio_Registers.html and https://gbdev.io/pandocs/Audio_details.html.
`host/apu.c` is written from those pages and says what it leaves out.

### The four channels

| Channel | Registers | What it makes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CH1 | NR10-NR14 (FF10-FF14) | a pulse wave with a frequency sweep |
| CH2 | NR21-NR24 (FF16-FF19) | a pulse wave |
| CH3 | NR30-NR34 (FF1A-FF1E), wave RAM FF30-FF3F | 32 four-bit samples, looped |
| CH4 | NR41-NR44 (FF20-FF23) | noise from a linear-feedback shift register (LFSR) |

**The pulses (CH1, CH2).**
The duty is NRx1 bits 7-6: 12.5%, 25%, 50% or 75%.
The period is 11 bits (NRx3 low 8, NRx4 bits 2-0); the tone is 131072 / (2048 - period) Hz, so 64 Hz to 131 kHz.
CH1's sweep (NR10) moves the period every pace / 128 s (pace 1-7) by period >> step (step 0-7), up or down; a sweep up past 2047 turns the channel off.

**The volume envelope (CH1, CH2, CH4).**
NRx2: an initial volume 0-15 (bits 7-4), a direction (bit 3) and a pace (bits 2-0): every pace / 64 s the volume moves one step, so the fastest fade from 15 to 0 takes 234 ms.
A new volume or envelope takes effect only on a trigger (NRx4 bit 7); writing NRx2 while the channel plays ("zombie mode") does different things on different models, so the driver never does it.

**The length counters.**
Every channel can stop itself: NRx1 bits 5-0 for CH1, CH2 and CH4 give (64 - L) / 256 s, CH3's NR31 gives (256 - L) / 256 s, counted when NRx4 bit 6 is set.
That is 3.9 ms resolution, four times finer than a 60 Hz frame, and it is how the driver ends a landing inside its tick.

**The wave channel (CH3).**
32 four-bit samples, played at 2097152 / (2048 - period) Hz (a tone of 65536 / (2048 - period) Hz).
No envelope: NR32 bits 6-5 give mute, 100%, 50% or 25% (a shift of the 4-bit sample).
On the DMG the wave RAM is reliably reachable only while CH3 is off (NR30 bit 7, its DAC, cleared); and a trigger plays the sample already in its buffer before the new table's second sample, so a reloaded table starts one sample late.

**The noise channel (CH4).**
NR43: the LFSR clocks at 262144 / (r x 2^s) Hz, r = NR43 bits 2-0 (0 counts as 0.5), s = bits 7-4 (14 and 15 stop it), so 524 kHz down to 2 Hz.
Bit 3 picks a 15-bit LFSR (noise) or a 7-bit one (a buzzy tone, the 127-step loop).
The output holds each pseudo-random bit for one clock, so its spectrum is sinc^2: flat up to about half the clock, falling to a null at it.
A slow clock is a dull, low noise; a fast one is a bright hiss, quieter in the audible band (at 524 kHz only 6% of its power is below 16 kHz).
A trigger resets the LFSR, and its output then holds for 15 clocks (the reset run): 5.5 ms at a 2.7 kHz clock, longer than a 241 Hz tick, which is what broke the first fit (see "The card sounds").

**The DACs, the mix and the output.**
Each channel has a 4-bit DAC, on while NRx2's top five bits are not all zero (CH3: NR30 bit 7); switching a DAC off or on mid-play is a pop.
NR51 sends each channel to the left, the right, both or neither: hard panning, nothing in between.
NR50 sets a master volume a side, 3 bits ((v + 1) / 8), and NR52 bit 7 powers the APU (its low bits say which channels are on).
The output goes through a capacitor (a high-pass near 30 Hz: Pan Docs gives the charge factor 0.999958 a 4.19 MHz cycle).
The frame sequencer clocks length at 256 Hz, the sweep at 128 Hz and the envelopes at 64 Hz.

### Playing samples

The APU has no sample channel, and every way to play a recording costs an interrupt per few samples.

| Way | How | Quality | CPU |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CH3 reload | every 32 samples: DAC off, 16 bytes into wave RAM, DAC on, retrigger | 4 bits; a pop and a one-sample slip at every reload (a buzz at the reload rate); at 8192 Hz, nothing above 4 kHz | 256 interrupts a second at 8192 Hz: about 9.8% of the CPU in C through GBDK's dispatcher, 3.7% in an assembly handler on its own vector |
| NR50 master volume | all channels held at a constant level, NR50 written once a sample | 3 bits (8 levels), every channel scaled at once, so nothing else can play | one interrupt a sample: 8192 a second is about 23% of the CPU, and every interrupts-off window is an audible hole |

Both are hurt by the time the game spends with interrupts off.
Measured from the ROM as it is (`build/durak.map` and the bytes at `_memcpy`, 0x2E1D: an unrolled copy, 7 M-cycles a byte):

| Window | Length | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the sprite staging copy (`gfx_spr_end`, 160 bytes, `CRITICAL`) | about 1,120 M-cycles, 1.07 ms | every frame that draws sprites: every frame of a flight, the cursor, the fireworks |
| `from_bank` (256-byte pieces with `di`) | about 1,800 M-cycles, 1.7 ms a piece | screen loads, with the LCD off |
| GBDK's VBlank handler (OAM DMA, `sys_time`) | about 250 M-cycles | every frame |
| GBDK's interrupt exit | waits for STAT mode 0 or 1, up to about 60 M-cycles | every interrupt |

At 8192 Hz a sample is 128 M-cycles, so a reload held by the staging copy is up to 9 samples late, and the channel replays the old table's start meanwhile.
`host/render.c` models exactly these windows for approach B, so its renders carry those slips.

## Sound and the game, one machine

### Memory: one owner at a time

Alex: memory can be shared by completely different things depending on what is needed; no sound plays while a bot thinks with that memory, and while a sound plays no bot needs it.

So the driver's 53 bytes of working state (`snd_state` in `snd.c`: four voices of 7 bytes, a strike queue of 8 x 3 bytes, the loaded wave table's index) are a member of a union over the search bots' scratch.
The scratch today is `gd_bot_world` (0xC0B1), `gd_bot_cand` (0xC11D, 228 bytes) and `gd_bot_sim` (0xC201, 99 bytes), shared already by Seoul and Moscow (`gd_botlib.c`).
Both rebuild the candidate list from zero on every decision (`nc = 0` in `candidates`) and copy the chosen move out to the caller's stack before returning, so nothing in it outlives a think; the union goes over `gd_bot_cand`.
Five bytes cannot be overlaid and go in HRAM (19 of its 127 bytes are used today, `_HRAM 0x13`): `snd_owner`, `snd_refused`, `snd_starts`, `snd_active` and the round-robin counter.
Work RAM cost: zero bytes, so the 80 bytes of stack headroom are untouched.
The driver adds one frame to the interrupt's stack (its locals: `add sp, #-11` in `snd_tick`), about 15 bytes over what `in_tick` already uses; the build phase reads the stack's deepest reach again (README "RAM") and must still find at least 60 bytes spare.

The other places looked at, and why they are not needed:

- The solver's cart RAM (32 KB, MBC1 banks 0-3) would hold sound data only while no solve runs, but neither approach needs RAM for data: approach A reads its rows from ROM, and approach B streams its samples from ROM 16 bytes at a time into wave RAM, with no buffer in between.
  It stays the solver's, and an interrupt never switches RAM banks under it.
- The sprite staging buffer (160 bytes) is in use exactly when sound is (every frame of a flight), so it cannot be shared.

The overlay changes nothing in the A against B verdict below: RAM was never what limited PCM, the CPU, the interrupts-off windows and the quality are.

### The phases, and who owns what

| Phase | Sound may strike | The scratch belongs to | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| the dashboard | yes (no cue today) | sound | |
| the table: flights, landings, the bot's half-second wait | yes | sound | every card cue |
| Seoul or Moscow thinking (`bot_thinking = 1`, the screen still) | no: refused and counted | the bot | the main loop is blocked in `gd_seoul_choose` or `gd_moscow_choose`; the timer interrupt still runs, and its sound tick returns after one load |
| New York or Miami deciding | yes | sound | they never touch the scratch (New York's state is its own, `gd_hw_*`), and take 28 ms |
| the win and lose screens | yes | sound | FINISH or FOOL, the fireworks |

**How the code enforces it.**
One owner byte, `snd_owner`, in HRAM.
Before Seoul or Moscow think, `bot_turn` waits for `snd_busy()` to be 0 (returning to the main loop each frame meanwhile, as it already does while `idle < BOT_DELAY`), then calls `snd_yield()`: every channel silenced, the queue dropped, the owner set to the bot.
After the move is chosen and before `play()` strikes its cues, `snd_claim()` starts the driver clean (its state was overwritten by the search) and hands the RAM back.
While the bot owns it, `snd_strike` refuses and counts (`snd_refused`); with every voice and strike dropped, the next tick clears `snd_active` and every tick after returns at once.
The tick does not test the owner as well: that second guard would cover nothing the first does not (a mutant that removed it survived every test), so there is one rule with one owner, the strike's refusal.
A gbrun check holds it (see "Tests"): `bot_thinking` and `snd_active` are never 1 in the same frame, and `snd_refused` is 0 at the end of every scripted game.

**What a cue does that would fire mid-think.**
It cannot be struck: the main loop, the only place that strikes, is inside the think.
A cue still sounding when the think would start is the real case, and the timeline answers it (frames from the move, the frame `play()` runs; a flight is 12 frames; the bot acts after `idle` reaches 30, and `idle` counts loop passes of at least a frame each, so the think starts at frame 30 at the soonest; times from `snd_pre` and `snd_dur` in `snd_tab.c`, one tick a frame, plus the one tick a strike waits in the queue):

| Cue | Struck at | Over at | Before frame 30? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LIFT | 1 | 14 | yes |
| PLACE or COVER, 1 card | 11 | 24 to 25 | yes |
| PLACE or COVER, 2 cards | 11, 14 | 28 | yes |
| PLACE or COVER, 3 to 6 cards | ... 17 to 26 | 31 to 40 | no: up to 10 frames late |
| PASS (from 249 ms after the landing) | 28 | 55 | no: 25 frames late |
| PICKUP | 1 | 25 | yes |
| INTO_HAND | 12 | 39 | no: 9 frames late |
| SWEEP, then PILE (a bout ending, no flight) | 1, 17 | 28, 34 | no: 4 frames late |

So a short cue is over before the think, and four are not; for those the bot waits, by at most 25 frames (0.42 s, a transfer), usually not at all.
I chose the wait over cutting the sound: the board has been still for half a second and the rail is about to say THINKS, so the end of a slide is the only thing happening, and cutting it would be heard.
The yield still cuts anything left, so a future cue that runs long cannot break the rule, only shorten itself.

**Refills and the deal.**
The deal is not animated yet (README "Not done"); when it is, its flights must keep `idle` at 0 until the last card lands (one card in flight at a time, 8 frames a card, art README "The deal"), so the bot's wait counts from the end of the deal, and the deal's cues are over by the rule above.

### The interrupt it runs on

The timer interrupt the game already has: `in_tick` at 4096 / (256 - 188) = 60.2 Hz (`input.c`), chosen because it keeps running with the LCD off.
`in_tick` calls `snd_tick()` after reading the pad; there is no new interrupt, no new vector, and no change to the timer.
A tick is 0.8% longer than a frame, so over a 12-frame flight a landing sound drifts by a tenth of a frame against `sys_time`: nothing.
The driver never disables interrupts, and an interrupts-off window (1.07 ms at most outside screen loads) only delays a tick by that much: a row is never lost, since the timer's flag waits.

### 60 Hz or 241 Hz

The fit was made both ways (`make tables` writes both; `build/gb_a241` is the 241 Hz render): 241 Hz is TMA 239 with the input run every fourth tick.

| | 60 Hz | 241 Hz |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mean envelope error, landings / the rest | 4.5 / 5.8 dB | 4.1 / 5.4 dB |
| tables | 4,962 bytes | 13,224 bytes |
| interrupts a second while sounding | 60 (already there) | 241, and the input divided down |

0.4 dB is not worth a new timer rate, four times the dispatch cost, and three times the tables, so 60 Hz.
Alex may hear it differently: the 241 Hz renders are in `build/gb_a241` beside the 60 Hz ones.

### The bots' think time

Neither approach plays during a think, by the owner rule, so neither slows one.
What a think pays is the idle tick: about 27 M-cycles, 60 times a second, 0.14% of the CPU: 7 ms of Moscow's 4.84 s average decision.
Approach B, if it ran its own 256 Hz interrupt, would have to set the timer back to 60 Hz before every think, or pay its dispatch cost (about 100 M-cycles a reload) through it: 2.4%, 117 ms a decision.
The wait for a still-sounding cue (above) delays the start of a think by up to 0.42 s on a transfer and by nothing on most moves; it is time the player spends listening, not time the bot spends searching.

### ROM

The cart is MBC1, 256 KB, 16 banks; banks 0 to 8 are used (README's table), 9 to 15 are free (112 KB), and bank 0 has 4,529 bytes free above `_CODE` (0x0800 + 0x264F).
The driver's code is 1,495 bytes (SDCC, `_CODE 0x5D7`); it is called from the interrupt, so it goes in bank 0.
The tables (4,962 bytes) go in bank 9, which bank 0 cannot also hold beside the driver; in the build phase `snd_tick` switches to it and back (`_current_bank` saved and restored, about 10 M-cycles), which is safe because code that maps another bank with interrupts on goes through `SWITCH_ROM`, and the tick puts back what it found (the one place with interrupts off, `from_bank`, cannot be interrupted).
Approach B's 45,660 bytes of PCM would fill banks 9 to 11.

**The website's rules, kept.**

- The stagger: the strikes of a beat are 3 ticks apart (49.8 ms, the website's 45 ms rounded to the tick), from the landing on (`SND_STAGGER`).
- The cap: at most 6 strikes a beat (`SND_MAX_HITS`), whatever the card count; the GB flies only 4 cards, and the rest land at once, but they still strike.
- A revert is the undone cue backwards (`SND_BACKWARDS`), with no take of its own.
- A sound trails its picture: every strike goes through the queue, so it starts on the next tick, never before; a landing's moment is the landing frame; a take-off cue's moment trails the card leaving.
- Takes are played in turn, never the same one twice running: a counter over a cue's three takes (the website draws; the GB needs no randomness at all, so there is none).
- A sound is played whole: a strike on a busy channel takes it only from a cue of no higher priority (a landing outranks a lift), and the cut voice is the older one.

**Level.**
The website's span (its loudest 50 ms RMS from -20 dBFS for the trump's landing to -54 for the deal's slide) is wider than 4-bit volumes and a small speaker carry, so it is mapped onto the Game Boy at GB = -14 + 0.6 x (web + 20) dBFS (`gen_tab.py gb_level_db`).
The tunes sit under the cards, as the website turned its placeholders down: their volumes are scaled by 0.55 (`JINGLE_GAIN`).
The quietest cues (INTO_HAND, LIFT) end up at volumes 1 and 2 of 15, where a step is 3 to 6 dB, which is why their level error is the largest (up to +7 dB on INTO_HAND).

**Panning.**
Everything is centred (NR51 0xFF): the website plays centred too, the DMG's speaker is mono, and its panning is hard left or right, nothing between.

## The card sounds: two approaches, measured

**(A) Synthesis.**
Every card take is fitted to the website's real take (`gen_tab.py fit_ticks`, `fit_events`):

1. The window: the part of the take the GB has time for (`CARDS` in `gen_tab.py`), at the GB's level.
2. Each tick: the LFSR clock whose sinc^2 spectrum is nearest the tick's own in 16 bands from 60 Hz to 15 kHz, and the tick's RMS.
3. Events: from each onset, the longest run of ticks one trigger can make within 2 dB (an initial volume, an envelope direction and pace, and, before a silence, a length to cut it inside its tick), with the clock moved live tick by tick inside it.
4. A trigger's reset run would make a slow clock silent for longer than a tick, so every event triggers at a clock of at least 75 times the tick rate (the run under a fifth of a tick) and moves to the clock its spectrum wants from its second tick on.
   The first fit, without this, retriggered at 2.7 kHz every 4 ms and was silent: PLACE came out 22 dB low.
   It is also why a landing's first tick is a bright click, which is what costs A its high-band error on PLACE (6.7 dB above 4 kHz).

**(B) PCM.**
The same windows, resampled to 8192 Hz, quantised to 4 bits (the NR32 level among 100%, 50% and 25% that keeps the most bits), played through CH3 reloads at 256 Hz with the reload's pop, its one-sample slip and the game's interrupts-off windows modelled (`host/render.c render_b`).

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DEAL | 8.9 | 3.2 | 4.8 | 7.4 | 3.0 | 4.5 |

**The recommendation: A, synthesis, for every cue.**
It is one engine on an interrupt the game already has, a twelfth of the ROM, no new timer, no pops, and it is closer to the website on every sound with air in it, which is most of them.
B's one win is the landings' low end, by about 2 dB; the measurement includes B's pops, so that win is real, but it is small next to a second engine, a 256 Hz interrupt and a buzz.
**The hybrid**, B for the five landings only (PLACE, COVER, FLIP, PILE, DEAL_LAND: 17 KB of PCM, the reload interrupt for 100 to 500 ms at a time), is the runner-up, and the one place where Alex's ear should decide: the preview plays each landing's A and B side by side.

## The driver

`snd.h` and `snd.c`, plain C, compiled for the Game Boy by SDCC and for the Mac by the renderer, so the preview runs the code that will ship.

**The data, a fixed binary layout (no JSON anywhere).**

```c
typedef struct { uint8_t t, r0, r1, r2, r3, r4; } snd_row;      // hold t ticks; t == 0 ends the voice
typedef struct { const snd_row *v[4]; } snd_take;              // a voice a channel, or 0
typedef struct { uint8_t prio; uint8_t n_takes; const snd_take *takes; } snd_cue;
extern const snd_cue snd_cues[SND_N_CUES];
extern const uint8_t snd_pre[SND_N_CUES], snd_dur[SND_N_CUES];
```

A row is the channel's own five registers: NRx0 to NRx4 (CH2 has no NR20; CH3's r0 is a wave table's index).
The rule, which keeps away from the DMG's model-dependent corners: r2 == 0 is a rest; a row with the trigger bit writes all five; any other row writes only r3 and r4 (a pitch or noise-clock change, live).
A rest retriggers at volume 0 with the DAC left on (NRx2 0x08), and the wave channel's rests mute NR32: the DACs go on once at `snd_init` and are never switched again, because a DAC switched mid-play pops.
The one exception is a wave table change, which needs CH3 off; it happens only at a trigger, under the new note's onset.
6 bytes a row, 8 a take, 4 a cue; 680 rows and 59 voice ends in all.

**The state** (53 bytes, overlaid): four voices (the row, the ticks left, the priority, the flags it was struck with, its first row for a backwards walk) and a queue of 8 strikes (the cue, the ticks to go, the flags).

**The tick** (`snd_tick`, in the timer interrupt): if a bot owns the RAM or nothing sounds, return; step each voice whose row is done to its next row (or its previous one, backwards) and write it; then start the strikes coming due.
The voices step before the strikes start, so a new row holds its whole `t`.

**How game code triggers a cue.**

```c
// main.c play(), after fly_to(): what a move sounds like (n = the cards it lands)
snd_strike(SND_LIFT, 0, 1, 0);                                         // take-off
snd_strike(SND_PLACE, FLY_FRAMES - snd_pre[SND_PLACE], n, 0);           // the landing, n strikes a stagger apart
if (m->type == GD_PASS) snd_strike(SND_PASS, FLY_FRAMES + 15, 1, 0);   // 249 ms after the landing
// a refill's card at the bot's seat
snd_strike(SND_DEAL_LAND, 8 - snd_pre[SND_DEAL_LAND], 1, SND_FAR);
// bot_turn(), Seoul and Moscow
if (snd_busy()) return;            // a cue still sounding: try again next frame
snd_yield();
gd_moscow_choose(&G, BOT, &m);
snd_claim();
```

The game says which cue and when (as the website's plan does); the driver says only what a strike writes, as the website's voice does.
The delay is in ticks, counted by the interrupt, so a slow redraw never makes a late sound.

**Assembly?**
Not yet: the C is about 1,000 M-cycles a tick while sounding, 5.7% of a frame for the few hundred milliseconds a cue lasts, and nothing during a think.
If the build phase's scanline clock finds it slower than 1,500 M-cycles a tick, the first step is the one the rollout engine took (README "The rollout engine"): the state as fixed-address arrays instead of a struct reached by pointer, which SDCC reaches far faster; assembly only after that, and only if measured.

## Listening

`make -C gameboy/audio preview` (about 20 s of one core) writes `build/preview.wav` and its listing `build/preview.txt`.
For each cue in the order a game plays them: the website's real take, then the Game Boy's synthesis (A), then its PCM (B) for the card cues, 0.35 s apart, 1 s between cues; then a short game in context at the frames the game will strike them (a deal, an attack of two, two covers, a good and a sweep, a pickup, a transfer, going out, Moscow's motif and three fireworks, the fool).
Every clip is brought to the same loudness so the three are compared by their sound; the in-context part keeps the tables' own levels.
The per-take renders are in `build/gb_a` (A at 60 Hz), `build/gb_a241` (A at 241 Hz) and `build/gb_b` (B), and the real takes' windows in `build/target`.

The final judge is Alex by ear.
`host/apu.c` is a model; mGBA's render of the real ROM comes in the build phase, and where the two differ, mGBA is the one to believe.

## Tests for the build phase

**Each cue fires at its frame (mGBA's audio).**
gbrun gets an `audio FILE` command (libmgba's audio buffer, read every frame into a WAV) and an `onset FRAME TOL` check on it: the capture's 5 ms envelope must rise 12 dB within TOL frames of FRAME.
A script per move (`scripts/sound_*.txt`): an attack of one, of two (two onsets 3 frames apart), a six-card throw-in (exactly 6 onsets), a cover, a transfer (PASS 15 frames after the landing), a pickup, a bout's end, going out, the win and lose screens.
The landing onset must be at the landing frame or up to 2 frames after it, never before (a sound trails its picture).
gbrun also reads the driver's queue and voices (as it reads `fly_pos`), so each cue's start tick is checked exactly, not only by ear.

**No audio work with interrupts off longer than 1.2 ms, and no lost tick.**
The driver has no `CRITICAL` and no `di` (`tests/snd_no_di.sh` greps `snd.c`, mutation checked by planting one).
A profiling ROM (as `durak_prof.gb`) records the longest gap between two timer interrupts on the line clock over the scripted games: it must stay under one tick plus 1.2 ms (the staging copy, 1.07 ms, the longest window outside a screen load); with the LCD off for a load it may reach 1.7 ms, the longest `from_bank` piece, and no tick may be lost (a count of ticks against `sys_time`).
The same ROM times `snd_tick` itself: under 1,500 M-cycles at its worst over every cue.

**Think-time regression.**
`make seoulcheck` and `make moscowcheck` must still match the clang build move for move with the driver linked: the sound draws no random number and touches no rules state.
`scripts/game_moscow.txt`'s times: the average decision within 0.5% of today's (4.84 s, so at most 4.87 s), the worst within 0.5% (5.99 s, so at most 6.02 s), measured from `snd_yield` to `snd_claim`; and the wait before a think (from the board's last change to `snd_yield`) at most 25 frames over `BOT_DELAY`.

**The owner rule.**
`notboth bot_thinking=1 snd_active=1` over every scripted game against Seoul and Moscow, and `snd_refused == 0` at the end of each.

**The driver's rules, now (`make -C gameboy/audio test`).**
`host/snd_test.c` drives `snd.c` on the Mac and checks its register writes: a strike starts on the next tick and never inside the call, two cards start a stagger apart, nine cards strike six times, a strike during a think is refused and counted and the tick writes nothing, `snd_claim` starts again, a backwards strike begins on the take's last row, and a far landing is at half volume.
`tests/mutants.sh` breaks each of those rules in a copy of `snd.c` and requires the test to fail: 8 mutants, 8 caught, each by the assertion for its own rule.

**Host checks to add in the build phase (cheap).**
`snd_tab.c` regenerates byte for byte (`make tables` twice, `cmp`); every card cue's `snd_pre` fits inside a flight (under 12 ticks); every card cue except those the timeline table lists is over by frame 30; the RNG ban (`tests/rng_ban.sh`) covers `gameboy/audio`.
