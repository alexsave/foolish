Correspondence Durak, in a bubble

Mockups for the Messages extension specced in docs/IMESSAGE_GAME_DESIGN.md: the game bubble (MSMessageTemplateLayout), the compact drawer, the expanded table, and the finished-game bubble. The visual language is the iOS/web app's — wool, wood, fern, Georgia card faces — with ONE borrowed idea from the watch study: at bubble size, cards render as rank-in-glyph marks (watch Option H's card language), because a 270 pt-wide transcript image cannot carry six legible card faces. All coordinates in iPhone SE points (375 × 667).

1 · The turn bubble

One bubble per game (MSSession replacement — older turns collapse to their summaryText, the live bubble drops to the bottom of the transcript). The bubble is the app's whole storefront: public table snapshot, whose-move caption, turn counter.

2 · Compact — the drawer

Keyboard-height presentation. No text input allowed here (platform rule), no horizontal scrollers. Two jobs only: resume a known game, start a new one. Tapping either requests .expanded.

3 · Expanded — the table

The full-height sheet. This is the phone app's Option-B table (see docs/ios-phone-layout.html) with three iMessage-specific deltas: a grabber + Messages nav peeking at the top, the action bar gains SEND MOVE (stages the bubble — the human always taps Messages' send), and a "staged" state that locks the board until didStartSending/didCancelSending resolves.

The one interaction that is new. In the app, playing a card IS the commit. In Messages, local play is provisional: apply moves optimistically via the kernel, then SEND MOVE stages one bubble carrying the whole updated chain, and the send button in the input bar is the real commit. The staged state must be visually unmistakable (dimmed board + banner) because a user who backs out silently cancels the turn (§17.2 — treat the cache as advisory, trust only tapped payloads).

4 · Game over — the funnel bubble

The FINISHED bubble's message.url is the standard v5/v6 replay link (foolish.cards/<code>), not /m/ — every tap on any platform lands on the replay page where the Oracle lives. This one line is the whole ecosystem funnel (§12).

5 · Group lobby & the /m/ fallback

Two more surfaces the extension must ship: seat-claiming in a group thread (WAITING phase — zero kernel actions, so claim races merge trivially), and the web page every Mac/Android/SMS recipient lands on (§13 of the spec: it IS the non-iPhone experience, not polish).

6 · Mock-to-build notes

  • Bubble snapshot renderer: a pure SwiftUI view (public GameView → glyph battle rows + count chips on wool) rendered by ImageRenderer/UIGraphicsImageRenderer at 300×195@3x. Reuses FoolishKit tokens; add the wool CGContext generator (same porting pattern as FernCardBack.swift).
  • Glyph card: new tiny FGlyphCard in DesignSystem (suit SF-symbol silhouette + rank overlay at 54% height, heavy weight) — shared by this bubble, future watch build, and any small-surface render. One component, three platforms.
  • Drawer rows come from the App Group cache ({game_id → mySeat, maxTurnSeen, finished}, §9.3); conversation names are NOT available to the extension — rows are "Game from July 12 · turn 24 · your move".
  • Expanded board is TableView from FoolishKit Boards with a reduced chrome flag — do not fork the board; the v1 UI cap is 4 players (§2), so the arc logic already covers it.
  • Staging: conversation.insert(message) ONLY stages; flip UI to "staged" and resolve on didStartSending/didCancelSending (§11.4, §17.1–17.2).
  • Dark mode: the wool table is self-luminous — identical in both modes; only Messages chrome (bubble grays, drawer) follows the system. Frames M2/M5 show it.
