# CL-20: results of a retired bot

CL-20 was a C-only research bot, forked from octogen on 2026-09-07 to beat it on Elo.
It was built on `claude/octogen-elo-bot-inl6k2` (18 commits, tip `8bfcd2a`), and retired and deleted on 2026-11-26.
Fusion ([FUSION.md](../c/FUSION.md)) is the rung above octogen.

## Result

CL-20 beat octogen, by about four Elo.

| Arena | Games | Elo gap over octogen (95% CI) | Direct head to head |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eleven bot pool, 6 seeds | 120,000 | +3.5 (+0.6 to +6.5) | 50.69% of 23,918 |
| Octogen and CL-20 only, 2 seeds | 30,000 | +3.8 (+1.3 to +6.2) | 50.54% of 180,525 |

Player counts were 2 to 8, rated by Bradley-Terry maximum likelihood with a game level bootstrap.
For scale, octogen's gap over cordite in the same runs was about +12.
Paired on the same deals, no cell of players 2, 3, 4 and 6 against six fields differed significantly from octogen.

## The three changes that paid

Deltas are mean finishing place against an octogen control on the same deals, and negative is better.

- Weak seat profiling (octogen's `OG_PROFILE`, switched on): -0.020 +/- 0.012 at 4 players and -0.035 +/- 0.017 at 6 against random seats, 600 pairs.
  It flagged 0.0% of handwritten, espresso and robusta seat decisions, and 3% of cordite's.
- A rollout model of seats proven strategic, which picks up while holding a full cover 30% of the time when the cover needs a trump and 15% otherwise.
  About -0.02 pooled heads up against octogen, a wash against cordite.
  Adding declined throw ins, declined transfers or a different first attack did not improve on pickups alone.
- Three times the sampled worlds at 6 or more players: about -0.05 +/- 0.03 at 6, none measurable at 5 or 8, at 3x the decision cost.

Measured behavior with the deck alive, in self play:

| Bot | Picks up holding a non trump cover | Cover needs a trump | Declines a legal throw in | First attack differs from handwritten |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| octogen | 15% | 31% | 32% | 54% |
| cordite | 15% | 23% | 36% | 64% |
| semtex | 22% | 32% | 38% | 63% |
| blackpowder | 23% | 26% | 39% | 89% |

## Dead ends

Each was implemented and measured, mostly at 200 to 300 pairs, and gave no gain.

1. Exact hand constraints from handwritten policy consistency: 0 violations in 3,909 constraints, null at every player count (about -0.01 +/- 0.015 pooled).
2. Extending the final two candidate duel until it separates: null.
3. Pruning candidates statistically with a higher world cap: unreliable, +0.095 +/- 0.062 worse in one cell.
4. Searching our own first decision inside the rollout: +0.170 +/- 0.072 worse against handwritten at 4 players, because it exploits sampled hidden cards.
5. Modeling our own seat as the strategic defender: +0.150 +/- 0.087 worse.
6. Espresso rollouts heads up: +0.060 +/- 0.046 worse.
7. Soft voids for strategic seats: +0.070 +/- 0.038 worse.
8. Keeping more candidates alive at 6 players: +0.035 +/- 0.109. Worlds are the limit there, not breadth.
9. Exact evaluation with a live deck: over 90% of solves abort at 100,000 nodes, at about 10x the decision cost.
10. Ten times the exact solve budget with a 36 card window: -0.010 +/- 0.023 at about 3x cost.
11. Counting handwritten violations as weakness: it flagged cordite 48% and robusta 43% against random's 29%.

## Where octogen has headroom

Six times the worlds, 200 pairs per cell:

| Table | 2 players | 3 | 4 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cordite | -0.025 +/- 0.043 | -0.100 +/- 0.069 | +0.060 +/- 0.090 | -0.280 +/- 0.109 |
| handwritten | | -0.030 +/- 0.049 | -0.075 +/- 0.064 | +0.000 +/- 0.105 |

Two to four players are saturated by search, and the 6 player mirror is limited by compute.

## Measuring effects this small

At 200 pairs the standard error is 0.02 to 0.03 heads up and 0.09 to 0.14 at six players.
Such a cell cannot resolve less than about 0.05 heads up or 0.25 at six, and one cell moved from +0.145 to -0.138 between two seed sets.
Decide on 600 to 1,000 pair cells.
