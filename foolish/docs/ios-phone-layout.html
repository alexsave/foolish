Foolish · design study · July 2026

Eight players on an iPhone SE

The offline-first phone table: the website's material world (wool table, wooden buttons, fern card backs) recomposed for the smallest supported iPhone — 375 × 667 pt, home button, no safe-area exotica. Three layout options; Option B ("Horseshoe") is the recommendation and is the layout the existing SwiftUI components in ios/FoolishKit/Boards/ should converge on. Companion decision record: docs/IOS_PHONE_LAYOUT.md. Engine facts cited there; nothing in these mocks invents a rule.

Scale
Real size assumes a 96-dpi CSS px; compare the bezel to a 67.3 mm-wide SE.

2 · Option A — "The website, verbatim" rejected

Port the web's radial PlayerRing (seats on an ellipse at 35% radius, self at bottom) straight onto the SE canvas.

Why it fails at 375 pt wide: the ellipse pushes seats 1 and 7 into the lower third of the screen, where they collide with the hand fan and the action buttons; the center battles zone gets squeezed to ~180 pt; and side seat names overlap the deck/discard wells. The web gets away with the ring because a desktop viewport is wide; portrait SE is not. Kept here as the fidelity baseline.

3 · Option B — "Horseshoe" recommended

The IOS_APP_DESIGN §6 banded layout ("opponents arced top, battles center, own fan bottom") realized with the website's materials. Opponents keep their seating order on a flattened arc — left neighbor at the left edge, right neighbor at the right — so the spatial "around a table" reading survives, but no seat ever drops below y≈150.

Why B wins. It is the only layout where all three variable-size regions (7 seats, N battles, M hand cards) degrade independently: seats compress along the arc, battles wrap and shrink, the fan overlaps tighter — no zone ever steals from another. It preserves the web's spatial metaphor (you sit at the bottom of a table) and its material identity, and it maps 1:1 onto the components that already exist: FSeatBadge (arc), FBattleGrid (center), FDeckWell, FHandFan, FActionBar.

4 · Option C — "Strip" fallback

The watch's Option-H economy scaled up: a flat seat-count strip on top, battles as the whole midfield. Maximum board room, minimum table feel.

Viable — and it is the natural landscape and iPad-compact answer — but on the SE portrait canvas it spends its won space on emptiness in typical 3–4 pair rounds, and names disappear entirely (identity moves one tap away, which the phone, unlike the watch, does not need to do). Keep as the degradation target if Dynamic-Type-XL testing shows the arc chips can't hold names. A parallel design pass (the retired ios-se-layout.html study) independently arrived at this strip as its primary — evidence it is a sturdy fallback — and contributed the one-tap roster sheet (tap the strip/arc → full-name sheet with counts, flip, and who's out), which Option B adopts as the arc's tap-through detail regardless.

8 · Handoff notes

  • Components already exist. TableView.swift + FSeatBadge/FBattleGrid/FDeckWell/FHandFan/FActionBar are written (never compiled — see IOS_APP_DESIGN §17). This study changes their composition and skin, not their contracts: adopt the arc seat placement, the web material fills, and the zone frames above.
  • Materials: fern card back is already ported (FernCardBack.swift). Wool and wood are canvas-generated on the web (WoolBackground.tsx, WoodTexture.tsx); port both generators to CGContext exactly like the fern (draw once per seed, cache). Until then the flat FColor.table + vignette stands in — but the wool IS the brand; schedule it.
  • Seat arc math: seat i of k opponents at t = (i+1)/(k+1): x = 24 + 327·t, y = 152 − 104·sin(π·t), chip centered. At k ≥ 6, odd-index chips drop +16 pt (zigzag) so dense arcs stay legible; small k spreads naturally.
  • Defender shield replaces the web's center arrow (an arrow crossing a 235 pt-wide battle zone reads as clutter at SE size; the shield-on-chip is the watch-proven idiom).
  • All gating via kernel: button enable-states and card legality come from fio_legal_moves_json / fio_legal_from_packed_json — never a Swift boolean.
  • Bot names render via the display-mapping layer (docs/IOS_BOT_NAMING.md): strip %, map family → localized city, keep the instance number; the picker adds the km-to-Moscow flavor line.
  • Absorbed from the parallel SE study (retired ios-se-layout.html): the one-tap roster sheet (arc tap-through); press-drag fan scrub for 15+ card hands — slide a finger along the fan, the touched card lifts and magnifies, release (or click) arms it — which keeps a 19-card hand playable at ~17 pt exposure with no second row and no horizontal scroll; and arm-then-target covering (tap your card to arm, tap the open attack to place) as the precision alternative to drag.
