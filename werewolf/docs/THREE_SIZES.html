The shadow theatre:
paper, one lamp, and what it throws

Messages shows an extension three ways: as a bubble in the thread, as a drawer at keyboard height, and as the whole screen. This page takes the three sizes UI.html settled and changes what they are made of. The stone, the grain and the tallow are gone. The screen is a cloth with a lantern behind it, every seat is a puppet cut from black paper, and the initial is cut out of the puppet so the lantern shows through. By day the lantern is in front: the same paper, cream, with ink on it. The stone version is kept next door as THREE_SIZES_STONE.html.

The drawer is the product

Most turns are taken collapsed, because collapsed is one tap from the thread and the thread is where the argument is. So the drawer is not a preview of the full screen; it is the screen, with the full one as the place you go when you need a name you cannot remember. Everything below is drawn in that order.

Every bubble the game can send

Thirteen shapes. Each one names a seat in the third person, because a bubble is baked when it is inserted and looks identical on every phone that receives it. The count sits top right, the headline bottom left, and the one line under the image is all Messages renders outside it - a single truncating line at the system size.

The sky is the progress bar. Both orbs rise on the right and set on the left, and where they sit is how many of the living have moved. It is the one piece of state everybody wants constantly and nobody wants to ask for, and it costs nothing because the count already exists.
Nineteen near-identical night cards is the disguise working. Wolf and villager send the same card. The count and the moon are the only things that change, which is what stops them being interchangeable in a scroll-back.
Your own move gets nothing beyond sitting on the right. A mark on your own bubbles is a mark anyone reading over your shoulder can use.
The two endings are the only bubbles anybody screenshots, so they are the only two with a sky of their own. Blood moon for the wolves; the lit village for the village.

Roughly 390 by 290pt, with the thread above it. The rule for this size: one line of caps, one question, and one thing to do. Whatever does not fit is not squeezed in; it waits in the expanded screen, and the drawer says nothing about it.

Two rules carried over from foolish, verbatim

Within one kernel move, everything that moves goes at once; between moves, one after another. The tap is one move: edge, retitle, reflow, staged line, all together.

A surface must be on screen for its settle_ms before it may be put away. The sheet over the pack settles for 400ms so a shield tapped the instant it appears does not drop a sheet the eye never saw.

The channel grid

One move arrives seven ways, and each way is a different animation for the same event. This is the grid foolish filled in by shipping something that felt wrong, seven times. A cell that says nothing moves is a decision. Rows marked proposed are this page's; the rest are already in UI.html.

ChannelWerewolf eventWhat movesStatus
A · stagedTap a shieldThe edge brightens (SNAP). The question retitles (TURN). The drawer collapses and the staged line rides the bottom edge in. All at once.decided
B · releasedThe native arrow sendsNothing, in the app. The bubble leaving the field is Messages' own animation and the drawer is already collapsed. The staged line fades to "sent" in place.proposed
C · own bubble, coldReopen a bubble you sentNothing moves. The board shows the state as of that bubble, with your shield's edge lit. A replay of your own move should not surprise you.proposed
D · a stranger's bubble, coldOpen someone else's bubbleNothing moves either - the sky in the bubble already was the replay. The board opens on the current state, not that bubble's, because a stale board at night is a board that leaks who has moved since.proposed
E · arrivalA bubble lands while the board is openDay: the named shield's tally ticks and the shield TURNs, 320ms. Night: nothing moves, ever - an arrival at night is a count.proposed
UndoThe x on the staged bubbleThe chosen edge goes dark (SNAP) and the question turns back from "Bram." to "Name one." Messages has already removed the bubble by the time the board hears, so this cannot be refused.proposed
ConflictThe night closed under you, or the vote order moved onThe whole surface cross-fades to the new state (FADE, 400ms) and the question says what happened: "The pack moved on." Never a dialog.decided

Why the night row says "nothing moves" so often

Every animation is something a shoulder can see. A shield that lights up when a bubble lands at night is a shield that lights up when a wolf moves, and a villager's board that does the same is a villager's board that has to fake it. So at night the board does not react to arrivals at all, for anybody. The day inverts that because a vote is public by definition.

What this page changes, and what it does not

The material

A lit screen and black puppets. The night is a cloth with a lantern behind it, amber in the middle and black at the edges. A seat is a heater cut from black paper, held a little off the cloth, so it throws a soft shadow. No noise, no gradient standing in for metal.
The initial is a hole, and the lantern shows through it. Every letter glows amber. The seat you chose is pressed flat against the cloth: no shadow, a hard rim of light, and its letter burns white. Same mechanism as the moon behind the village.
The wolves' room is lit red. The pack screens are the same lantern behind a red gel. A villager never sees it, so it can be as different as it likes.
The dead are cut out. Where a seat was there is only the cloth, a dashed line where the scissors went, and a name struck through. Reads at 37.5pt without a word.
Day is the same screen lit from the front. Cream paper, ink on it, hard shadows down and to the right. A chosen seat lifts off the wall instead of lighting up, because on a lit screen nothing can outshine the lamp.
The bubble is a paper theatre. Two hills of dark paper, the village cut from a third, a flat two-tone sky, and the moon or sun as the lamp behind it all, with a wide soft halo. The count and the caption are unchanged.
Everything written is on a torn strip - the pack's field, the staged line - and everything red is red paper: the door, the pack, the chosen name.

Changed against UI.html

The drawer shows the roster and the staged line together. The old "three states" section drew the drawer as a question and one row, before tapping was decided to collapse it. After the tap the drawer is where you are, so it has to hold both.
Dense tallies are corner numerals. The old page had no vote at all when the dense grid was drawn. There is no line under a 37.5pt shield to draw strokes on, so the count sits in the shield's square corner, in the same red as the strokes.
The pack has a drawer. Two lines, the field and the door fit in 290pt, which means a wolf can run a whole night without expanding, the same as a villager.
The chosen name takes the door's place. Once the kill is named the door has done its job; the name goes where the door was, so the layout does not grow.
Every element has an anchor, and the transitions are named from anim_plan.h. Neither existed on the old page.
One stylesheet. The forty rounds of !important are folded into the rules they overrode. Where two rules disagreed, the later one won, which is what the cascade was already doing.

Kept exactly

The shield, its traced outline, and its four states. Candlelit stone at night, the same stone under daylight by day, the cellar for the hall. Cinzel over EB Garamond and nothing under 11pt. The bubble's sky, count, headline and one-line caption. No Send anywhere. No count of who has yet to move, at night, on any screen. Pack is red, always.

Open, and not guessed at

Twenty is the switch from named to dense, on the arithmetic - seven columns of 44pt is 280pt in a 177pt slot at twenty seats. It has not been held.
Whether the drawer scrolls. The dense grid fits thirty-two, so the drawer never scrolls in this drawing. If the switch moves above twenty, it has to.
The per-player vote is still the kernel gap. Every day screen here is built on tallies the kernel cannot yet produce; ww_day_lynch models the result, not the vote. First thing to write.
Timings are guesses until the rig reads them off AnimLog. Every number in the tables above is a starting point, not a measurement.
