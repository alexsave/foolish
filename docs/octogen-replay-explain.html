How this was reproduced (read me)

Self‑play from the seed does not reproduce this game — p0 diverges on its very first response, so p0 was not this octogen (a human or other bot). The supplied 32‑byte deal seed also does not reproduce the recorded draw order under the native engine (it matches only the first ~27 logs by loose coincidence, then p0 is dealt a card the seed puts deep in the deck). So the deck was reconstructed directly from the recorded moves (the 36‑card set is known; every card p1 ever holds is eventually revealed, and p1 empties its hand at the end, so p1's hand is pinned exactly at every turn). Injecting that reconstructed deal into the engine and driving the recorded moves reproduces all 131 logs move‑for‑move. octogen is then queried at each p1 turn on the true state; its deliberation is faithful, deterministic, and does not perturb its own choice.

Rank convention is the correct Durak one (wire value 13 = A): the bundled replay_pretty.txt renderer is off‑by‑one (it prints A as K, etc.) — this page and the task descriptions use the engine's correct mapping, so a card shown here as A♠ is {suit:0,value:13}.

Notes & findings

  • Log 31 (flagged) — covering J♥ with the trump Ace is cosmetically odd but materially neutral. octogen would use the Queen; the two single‑covers are a near‑tie in MC — the recorded ace‑cover scored 1.4271 (192 sims) vs octogen's queen‑cover 1.4329 (864 sims), i.e. the ace‑cover was marginally lower but got pruned before the final stage, so octogen's queen preference is noise‑level. Because the whole pile is picked up next turn, the covering trump returns to hand — no trump is actually lost. p1 did hold trumps (Q♠, A♠) able to cover the 10♦ (even both attacks at once), but leaving 10♦ and picking up was correct (log 32: pickup 1.4155 beats cover‑10♦ 1.4537).
  • Log 116 (flagged) — the three‑Ace attack is a sound unload, not a waste. The exact solver could not resolve this 6‑vs‑6 attack position even at 200M nodes, so octogen used Monte‑Carlo, which scored the A♣ A♠ A♦ attack a certain win (finish 1 in all 864 sampled worlds). Three same‑rank Aces are a legal multi‑card attack that p0 cannot cover; p0 picks up, and p1 sheds its high cards on the way to the win.
  • Endgame solver works where the tree is small. By log 127 (p1 holds a lone 6♠) the solver proves the position: cover 6♠→A♣ = win, pickup = loss. octogen takes the proven win.
  • p1 is only partly this octogen build. Across 37 p1 turns octogen agrees on 22 (7 of them forced single‑move). The 15 disagreements — including the log‑31 Ace‑cover — mean the recorded p1 was not consistently this octogen configuration. The panels still show octogen's honest evaluation of each true position.
