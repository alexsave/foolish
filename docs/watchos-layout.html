Durak on a 40 mm wrist

Layout options for a watchOS client of the Foolish engine — 8 players, free-for-all throw-ins, transfer rules, no round timer. Every rule claim was read out of the engine (cnitro/src/game.c, legal.c, the wasm bridges), and every sizing claim is held to Apple's Human Interface Guidelines (quoted in §3 — 12 pt text floor, crown-first navigation, single-screen interactions). Mockups are HTML at native watch pixels; use the controls to view them at true physical size.

Your brief, corrected #1 — “max table slots”: there is no slot grid to design. The cap is the defender's live hand size (game.c:557-559), so the table view must be a scrollable strip/carousel that survives 10+ pairs, not a fixed 6-slot mat.
Corrected #2 — “timer, passes, or defender beats last pair”: no timer exists (good.ts:7-8). The everyday close is unanimous explicit GOOD, reset by any table change. “Defender beats the last pair” only closes the round in the special case that the play empties their hand (game.c:657). Consequence: GOOD is a first-class, always-reachable button — it is load-bearing, not chrome.
Corrected #3 — “duplicate card in hand”: impossible by construction (game.c:305-316, rearrange.ts:23-32); that screen state is cut. The real multi-card states the UI must carry instead: leading several same-rank cards (game.c:549) and multi-card transfer (game.c:724) — both get a two-button count sheet because they are the only moves that must be atomic.
Confirmed — “it's a race”: between attackers only. Actions serialize server-side under a version fence (utils.ts:63, utils.ts:829-837); the loser gets a typed reject — DEFENDER_CAPACITY, or STALE_ROUND if the round closed first (rejectMessages.ts:16,30-39). Covers cannot be sniped (only the defender plays them, targeting exact cards, game.c:621-629); what a throw-in can snipe is attack capacity and the defender's transfer window (game.c:734-735). So: optimistic send + honest toast, never a blocking confirm.

Everything follows from column three: a finger is 40% of the screen wide, so no more than two targets may share a row and three a column. Anything finer — picking one of six cards, one of ten table pairs — must go through the Digital Crown. The system clock always owns the top-right corner on watchOS; the top band is designed around it. Corners are real: the 60 px radius eats the corner cells, so nothing rectangular lives within 18 px of them.

3 · What Apple actually says

Quoted from the Human Interface Guidelines (fetched, not remembered). These four ideas invalidate parts of every board-shaped design below — and they produce Option D.

“People glance at the Always On display many times throughout the day, performing concise app interactions that can last for less than a minute each. People frequently use a watchOS app's related experiences — like complications, notifications, and Siri interactions — more than they use the app itself.” HIG · Designing for watchOS · “App interactions”
“Support quick, glanceable, single-screen interactions that deliver critical information succinctly and help people perform targeted actions with a simple gesture or two. Minimize the depth of hierarchy … use the Digital Crown to provide vertical navigation for scrolling or switching between screens.” HIG · Designing for watchOS · “Best practices”
“Starting with watchOS 10, the Digital Crown takes on an elevated role as the primary input for navigation … within apps, people turn the Digital Crown to switch between vertically paginated tabs.” “Anchor your app's navigation to the Digital Crown … back them up with corresponding touch screen interactions.” HIG · Digital Crown

4 · Option D — “One thing at a time” recommended

The watch is not a board renderer; it is a decision surface. Every choice is a full-bleed, crown-paginated screen: turn the crown through [TABLE] → [your cards, best first] → [GOOD], tap anywhere to commit the visible page. The app opens directly on your best legal card, so the common race play is zero detents + one tap. Layer 0 — notification, Smart Stack widget, complication — covers the 90 % of an 8-player game when it isn't your moment, exactly as the HIG prescribes.

Live demo — crown pages, tap anywhere

Hover the watch and scroll (or ↑↓ after clicking it) to page vertically: table → cards (legal first) → GOOD. The strip does not wrap — pages have ends, like a watchOS 10 TabView, with edge dots as the pagination indicator.

Tap anywhere on a legal card page to throw it (~1 in 3 loses the race and shows the DEFENDER_CAPACITY banner). Illegal pages state their reason in the bottom strip and ignore taps. The GOOD page commits the round-close vote and locks.

Legality still comes per card from guards.wasm gates (canAttack([card]), clientGuards.ts:219) — this option changes presentation, not the rules plumbing.

Verdict: the only design that satisfies the HIG numbers — every string ≥ 24 px (12 pt), body voice 25–33 px, one tap zone per screen — while getting faster for the race (opens on your best card; whole-screen commit can't be fumbled). Costs: the table is a page rather than a pane (mitigated: capacity and live values ride the top overlay on every card), and long post-pickup hands mean more detents (mitigated: legal-first ordering keeps playable cards at the front).

5 · Option B — “Focus flow” the dense compromise

The first 40 mm attempt: one decision per screen, crown-driven carousel, three fat tap zones. Kept because its interaction grammar (crown = aim, tap = commit) survives into Option D — but its information density does not: see the type audit above. At true scale, half its text sits below Apple's 12 pt floor.

Live demo — attacker turn

Hover the watch and scroll (or use ←→ after clicking it) to turn the crown through your hand. Illegal cards stay in the ring but render dim with a reason — legality comes from the same gates guards.wasm exposes (canAttack([card]) per card, clientGuards.ts:219).

Tap the focused card to throw it in — the demo plays the optimistic-send flow, including losing the race (~1 in 3 sends hits DEFENDER_CAPACITY so you can see the snipe toast). Tap GOOD to feel the lock-until-table-changes rule.

Compare it with the Option D demo above: same rules, same crown grammar — but count what you can actually read at “Real size”.

Verdict: every tap target survives the 128 px finger and it scales to any table size — but it fails the typography floor on five elements, which is disqualifying at 40 mm. Its screen-state inventory (the 13 states below) carries over to Option D one-for-one.

6 · Option A — “Dense board”

The web layout miniaturized: persistent table strip + hand carousel + actions, all on one screen. Included because it's everyone's first instinct — and to show where it breaks at 40 mm.

Verdict: best situational awareness per glance, and viable on 44/46 mm. At 40 mm the pair chips are 66 px — half a fingertip — so defender targeting must fall back to the crown anyway, and two crown-scrollable strips on one screen fight for the one crown. Keep as the large-watch variant, not the baseline.

7 · Option C — “Action list”

Pure watchOS: every legal move is a full-width row in a crown-scrolled list. The move space is small enough per moment (throwable cards + GOOD; or covers-for-current-target + TAKE + PASS) that rows stay countable.

Verdict: bulletproof targets and free accessibility, but it throws away the card metaphor, and rows reorder under you as rivals' throw-ins change legality mid-race — the list jumps exactly when speed matters. Right answer for an accessibility mode or a v0 spike; wrong default.

9 · Cut list

If an element can't justify itself, it's gone. What didn't earn pixels:

  • 8-player avatar ring — seat identity only matters twice (who defends, who receives a pass); both are named in the context band. Full roster lives in the table peek footer.
  • Discard pile / counts — card-counting is the Oracle's job on web; on the wrist it's noise. Deck count survives (three characters, decides endgame urgency).
  • Animations / evwire stream — the watch renders snapshot trailers only (evwire.h:24-27 makes this legal); motion is a luxury the round-trip budget can't fund.
  • Chat, emotes, replay, Oracle — different session, different device.
  • Hand rearrange — rearrange.ts exists for the web; the crown carousel has no drag affordance and auto-grouping by rank is strictly better here.
  • Multi-card cover batching — the engine takes covers pair-at-a-time happily (game.c:638-655); sequential taps reach the same states, including the hand-empty instant win. Only lead-count and pass-count need atomic sheets.
  • “Slots” meter as a graphic — capacity is one number; it rides in the ticker as cap n.

10 · What the watch client actually consumes

  • State in: subscribe the player's player_views row — already per-seat masked server-side, decoded by the shared packed-game codec (player_views.ts:1-13). No per-frame masking logic on the watch.
  • Legality: embed guards.wasm (~23 KB, fixed memory, clientGuards.ts:1-27) — per-card gates paint the carousel; the same awire bytes validated locally are POSTed verbatim (awire.h:1-13).
  • Moves out: one POST per action; on reject, map ENGINE_REJECT_*/STALE_ROUND to the toast strings (rejectMessages.ts) and re-render the pushed snapshot.
  • No timers to render: nothing in the engine counts down (good.ts:7-8) — the only “hurry” signal is social, and the design leaves it that way.
