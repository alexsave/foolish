How to read the panels

  • octogen turns. The full deliberation, dumped from the C engine. Away from the endgame octogen samples many worlds (deals of the hidden cards) and rolls each candidate move to the end; the bar is its average finish (1 = out first = win, higher = worse, lower is better). Once the deck is empty it solves the position exactly (proven win/loss). It also shows what octogen actually knows about the hidden cards: the cards it has pinned to a seat (watched them pick up) vs the unknown pool it samples over. Moves tagged raced out were dropped early by octogen’s progressive widening — it stopped sampling them, so their few‑sim average is noise and isn’t what it chose from (the green bar is the best fully‑simulated move).
  • random turns. Random has no belief and no rollout — it just picks one of its legal moves uniformly at random. So its “reasoning” is the full menu of legal moves at that position, each with probability 1/N; the one it actually played is highlighted. That’s the whole story — the contrast with octogen’s panel is the point.
  • Trump‑keep tax. While the deck is alive octogen adds a small tax (trump‑keep) to any move that leads a trump, tipping near‑ties toward keeping it. Taxed rows show raw + tax = adjusted.
  • Determinism. octogen’s world‑sampling seed is a pure function of the public board (plus a server‑only secret), independent of the random bots’ draws. Replaying its exact recorded picks through the deployed wasm reproduces every decision bit‑for‑bit — so the panels below are precisely what the shipped bot computed, not an approximation.
