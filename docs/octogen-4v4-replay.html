How to read the panels

  • octogen turns (p0–p3). The full deliberation, dumped from the C engine. Away from the endgame octogen samples many worlds (deals of the hidden cards) and rolls each candidate move to the end; the bar is its average finish (1 = out first = win, higher = worse, lower is better). Once the deck is empty it solves the position exactly (proven win/loss). It also shows what octogen actually knows about the hidden cards: the cards it has pinned to a seat (watched them pick up) vs the unknown pool it samples over.
  • random turns (p4–p7). Random has no belief and no rollout — it just picks one of its legal moves uniformly at random. So its “reasoning” is the full menu of legal moves at that position, each with probability 1/N; the one it actually played is highlighted. That’s the whole story — the contrast with octogen’s panel is the point.
  • Trump‑keep tax. While the deck is alive octogen adds a small tax (trump‑keep) to any move that leads a trump, tipping near‑ties toward keeping it. Taxed rows show raw + tax = adjusted.
  • “octogen agrees / would differ.” This reconstruction re‑drives the recorded game through the deployed wasm; at each octogen turn its pick is compared to what was recorded. octogen is deterministic, so matches are bit‑exact; a rare differ is a Monte‑Carlo near‑tie (both scores shown).
