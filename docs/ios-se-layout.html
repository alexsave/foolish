8 players on an iPhone SE

The barebones iOS app plays offline against the bot roster on the smallest modern iPhone stage: 375×667 pt, home button, no safe-area tricks. This study fits the full 8-seat Durak table onto that stage — leaning on the website's board for layout and the watch study (docs/watchos-layout.html) for density lessons — and answers the two ship-order questions (watch later? network later?) with sources. Companion docs: docs/IOS_APP_DESIGN.md (the app), docs/IOS_BOT_NAMES.md (the city roster shown in every frame). Design only — no code has changed; §7 lists the deltas an implementer applies to the existing SwiftUI.

1 · The stage, and why the SE is the target

If it's tasteful on the SE it's tasteful everywhere — every other iPhone only adds points. The SE is also the honest worst case for one-handed reach: no Dynamic Island to push content down, but only 667 pt of height for five stacked zones.

Engine facts that shape this layout (same list the watch obeys, WATCHOS_SPEC.md §0): several seats can act at once, so there is no "your turn" banner to design around; rounds close only by unanimous GOOD; there is no table-slot cap — a post-pickup defender can face 10+ pairs; the flipped trump is public and always drawn last; hands can exceed a dozen cards after a bad pickup. The board must degrade gracefully, not assume 6 pairs and 6 cards.

3 · The layout fixed-pt bands, flexible middle

The current SwiftUI table splits by percentage (22/40/26). On the SE with 8 players that starves the battles. The SE-tuned layout pins the bands that have intrinsic size and gives every leftover point to the battle stage:

ZoneFrame (pt)Content & rules
Status bary 0–20System. The app is full-bleed felt behind it.
Seat stripy 28–100 (72) Up to 7 chips, 46 pt wide, centered. Chip = card-stack silhouette with the hand count (condensed 17 pt) + name (8.5 pt, tail-truncated) + role row (shield = defender, red ring = attacker, green ✓ = said good, dots = thinking). Eliminated seats at 40%. The whole strip is one tap target → roster sheet (SE4) — names are legible there, not here. With ≤3 opponents chips relax to 84 pt (the current FSeatBadge).
Battle stagey 100–424 (324), right inset 70 Pairs on a 3-column flow grid, cell 92×100: attack card 56×78, cover overlaps rotated 12°. Covered pairs desaturate; armed-card cover targets get a brass ring; in-flight cards render at 45%. ≤9 pairs: centered. 10+: top-align and scroll vertically under an edge fade (watch rule, scaled ×3).
Deck wellx 305–367, y 100–210 Deck as a fern back with the count numeral punched over it (condensed 24 pt), flipped trump peeking out rotated 90° beneath, discard count + trump glyph caption under. After the flip is drawn: bare suit glyph (watch rule).
Action bary 432–476 (44) Take / Done / Pass as full-height buttons, enable-states strictly from the kernel's legal menu. The contextually-primary verb is the red fill; the rest are outlines. Hidden verbs collapse (bar never shows a dead button).
Hand fany 480–667 (187) One arc, 66×92 cards, exposure = (375−32−66)/(n−1), floor ≈ 17 pt at 19 cards. Below ~26 pt exposure, press-drag scrub activates: sliding a finger along the fan lifts + magnifies the touched card in a peek bubble (SE-D demo); release plays/arms it. Tap still works at any density. Trump cards carry the brass dot; illegal cards dim while a card is armed.

One-handed reach honesty: everything time-critical (fan, action bar) lives in the bottom 240 pt. The battle stage is mid-screen — fine, cover-targeting is a deliberate act. The seat strip is top-screen but purely informational; its tap (roster) is never urgent.

5 · Live demo — fan scrub at 14 cards mouse / touch

The one novel interaction this layout adds. Slide across the fan: the touched card lifts and a peek bubble magnifies it; release (or click) arms it. This is what makes a 19-card post-pickup hand playable at 17 pt exposure without a second row or horizontal scrolling.

6 · Decisions & rejected alternatives

  • Seats in a strip, not arced around the table (⇐ web). The website arcs opponents around an oval — right for landscape desktop, wrong at 375 pt: arcing eats the battle stage's corners and turns seat labels into confetti. The watch already proved a count-first strip communicates 8 seats in one glance.
  • Count-first chips, names second. With the city roster (IOS_BOT_NAMES.md) names run long (Yekaterinburg, Brighton Beach, 상트페테르부르크). Truncated names stay on the chip for identity, but counts are the live signal; full names live one tap away in the roster sheet. Rejected: two-row seat strip (steals 40 pt from battles for data nobody scans mid-round).
  • 3-column battle grid, vertical scroll past 9 — never a pager, never horizontal (the watch's hard-won gesture lesson: one axis, one meaning; vertical = table). Rejected: shrinking battle cards to fit 12+ pairs statically (below ~48 pt attack cards, cover-targeting misses exceed Apple's 44 pt floor).
  • One fan row + scrub, never two rows (two rows occlude the action bar or halve the battle stage; scrub keeps card identity readable at any count and matches the watch's "one representation, magnify in place" fisheye principle).
  • Deck well pinned top-right of the stage, not a side column. The current SwiftUI gives the deck a full trailing column — at 375 pt that's 17% of the width for two numbers and a card. Pinning it over the grid's top-right corner returns a full battle column.
  • Chrome-free stays. No turn banners, no timers (none exist in the rules), no score header. The leave affordance is a 40%-opacity chevron in the status region with a confirm dialog (§5.5: confirmations only for destructive acts).
  • Portrait only. Landscape on an SE is 375 pt tall — the fan plus battles cannot coexist; the web serves landscape desktop already.

7 · Ship order: what can safely come later

Owner constraints: no physical watch for ~a month; iMessage is the next push; the barebones offline app ships first. Both "later" questions check out — with receipts:

QuestionAnswerEvidence
Can the watch app be added later without penalty? Yes. A watchOS app is a new target added to the existing Xcode project and ships inside a normal app-version update on the same App Store record — no new record, no migration, no user-visible seam. (This is unlike iMessage apps, where standalone-vs-bundled is a day-one trap — already handled: the extension is specced as bundled.) App Store Connect: add watchOS app information; Setting up a watchOS project; repo decision already recorded in WATCHOS_APP_PLAN.md ("ship after Milestone F") and IOS_APP_DESIGN.md §17.8
Anything to do NOW so the watch add stays cheap? Only hygiene, all already planned: keep rules in C (done by policy); keep FoolishKit's Engine/Net layers UIKit-free (the one exception, Haptics, is already flagged for a WatchKit backend in WATCHOS_SPEC.md §0); later add watchOS slices to make ios-lib. No design debt in this layout affects the watch. WATCHOS_SPEC.md §0, §12
Can network play be added later? Yes — it's already a seam, not a rewrite. The board renders any GameSession; the online session + protocol layer exist in the tree (written, pending first xcodebuild); the Home screen already gates the PLAY button on backend config and shows "online arrives in a later update" when unconfigured. Shipping offline-only v1 = ship without backend keys. Apple accepts offline-only games (4.2 substance = offline bots, replays, tutorial). The server needs zero changes on either timeline. ios/FoolishKit/Engine/GameSession.swift, ios/FoolishApp/HomeView.swift (Backend.shared.isConfigured gate), IOS_APP_DESIGN.md §17.5, docs/PROTOCOL.md
Barebones MVP cut for the first TestFlight Offline table (this layout) + win/rematch + tutorial + settings + local replay save. Defer: online (keys absent), camera QR scan, iMessage, watch, Oracle (flagged off by design). IOS_APP_DESIGN.md §14 A–C, §17

8 · Handoff — deltas to the existing SwiftUI nothing applied yet

The app's table (TableView.swift + Boards components) already implements the right architecture; this study changes proportions and adds density behaviors. The implementer's list:

  • TableView bands: replace the 22/40/26 percentage split with §3's fixed bands (seat strip 72 pt, action bar 44 pt, fan 187 pt, battles = the rest).
  • FSeatBadge compact variant: 46 pt chip (count-first, 8.5 pt name, role row) selected automatically when opponents ≥ 4; whole strip becomes a Button presenting the roster sheet.
  • Roster sheet (new, small): the SE4 bottom sheet — rows of full name + count + role marks, footer with deck/discard/flip + out list. Reuses FSeatBadge data; content mirrors watch RosterScreen §3.
  • FBattleGrid: 3-column flow layout, cell 92×100; centered ≤9 pairs, top-aligned vertical ScrollView + edge fades past 9; keep the 92×100 cell as the tap target (≥44 pt rule).
  • FDeckWell: move from trailing-column HStack into a top-right overlay of the battle stage; count numeral overlays the fern back; flip → bare glyph after it's drawn.
  • FHandFan: exposure compression to a 17 pt floor + press-drag scrub with the peek bubble (SE-D); keep plain tap; dim illegal cards while armed.
  • Home stepper: 1...3 → 1...7 opponents (engine seats 2–8; this layout is why the cap can lift), and OfflineConfig's comment updated to match.
  • Seat names: wire the city roster display per docs/IOS_BOT_NAMES.md (BotNames + seatNames — spec'd there).
  • Accessibility: chips get combined VoiceOver labels ("Moscow, 4 cards, defending"); scrub honors Reduce Motion (peek without lift animation); the battle grid remains navigable card-by-card.
