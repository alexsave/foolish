Werewolf · surface study · 19 September 2026

Seven phones, one night,
and nobody may look different

The protocol is settled and the kernel implements it. What a player looks at is not. This is a menu to choose from - every option is buildable against exports that already exist, drawn at real size, and costed honestly.

What is already decided

Not design choices. These fall out of the protocol and the kernel, and every option below has to hold them.

Every living player sends exactly one message per night

The whole trick. A wolf is not behaving unusually by sending, because sending is the only thing anybody does. Any design that lets a villager skip, or makes a wolf's turn look different, gives the game away - permanently, in the transcript.

One bubble per player. Never two.

The wolf line rides inside the same payload as the night choice, masked. Never a second message: a second message is a countable bubble, and three extra bubbles on a five-player night is three wolves.

Send is refused for the first ten seconds

ww_send_floor_remaining. It removes the only latency tell there is - answering instantly, which only a player with no decision can do. Settled at ten seconds.

No clocks, no timers, no server - and the window is ten minutes

Nobody is awake to run a timer. If the next player in rotation opens after ten minutes, they may act, and their message carries the skips for everyone late (ww_may_carry, ww_night_pass_for). The night advances because somebody played, not because a clock fired.

One minute was the original figure and it is too harsh for a game people check between meetings; ten is long enough that being carried means you really were away.

The last wolf in rotation decides, and the rotation advances

ww_night_decider, ww_night_order. If the deciding wolf is skipped, the kill falls to the most recent wolf who did choose. You cannot hold the night open for one player without announcing them.

There is no seat cap

The only ceiling is iMessage's own group size. Every layout below has to keep working as the table grows, which is why a fixed geometry like the round table is a liability rather than a style choice.

The envelope stays bounded by forgetting

A ten-player first night seals to 286 bytes and 464 base32 characters against roughly 1000 available. The chain grows only because it carries nights and days that are already resolved - and a closed night is a settled fact, not a pending one. Truncating at the last closed morning bounds the envelope permanently, instead of buying time with entropy coding.

The register

Villagers and wolves, torches and accusations. The surface is soot, tallow and old blood - not a settings screen with a dark theme. Three rules hold it together.

Light comes from somewhere

Every screen has one warm source, off-centre, and falls off into a vignette. Nothing is evenly lit, because evenly lit is what a form looks like.

Seats are shields, not rows

A heraldic escutcheon with an initial. Sent lights the rim; chosen lights the field; the pack is stained; the dead are broken - the shield cracks rather than greying out.

Grain, everywhere

A fractal-noise overlay on every surface at low opacity. It is the single cheapest thing that stops a dark screen reading as a gradient.

Type: inscriptional caps over an old-style serif

Cinzel for anything carved - the question, the seal, small-caps labels. EB Garamond for anything spoken. No blackletter: it is the obvious choice and it is illegible at 13pt on a phone, which is where most of this text lives. Nothing below 11pt anywhere, per the standing rule.

The screens further down are drawn in the candlelit treatment. These are the alternatives, each showing the identical night - night III, seven seats, Alex out, Sveta chosen, Lee in the pack - plus its bubble, because the bubble is what sells the game to people who have not opened it. Open, and not guessed at

  • Ten seconds is settled - the owner has confirmed the floor feels right. The carry gate moved from one minute to ten: a minute is harsh for a correspondence game somebody checks between meetings.
  • The URL budget has an answer: drop the history. A ten-player first night seals to 286 bytes and 464 base32 characters against roughly 1000. The chain only grows because it carries every night and day already resolved - and those are settled facts, not pending ones. Once a night is closed nobody needs its records to replay: the resolved state is the state. Truncating at the last closed morning bounds the envelope permanently instead of buying time with entropy coding.
  • Nobody has decided what a dead player sees. Spectating is in the view model and has no screen, and a dead player who can still read the thread is a real social problem in the physical game too.
  • What a dead player does is the remaining unknown - see above. Seat count is not open: there is no game cap, only iMessage's group limit.
